# Night Storm Demoscene

A dark, rainy night sky. Click, clap or shout, and lightning cracks across the clouds. A few seconds later, the thunder rolls in.

**▶ Live demo: https://mkeshav04.github.io/night-storm-demoscene/**

## Just one HTML file

The whole thing is a single file: `index.html`, about **57 KB**.

- No images
- No sound files
- No libraries, no frameworks, no build step

Everything you see and hear is generated by code, live, while you watch.

## Why

I was learning about the **demoscene**: an old computer art culture (it goes back to the 1980s) where people make real-time audiovisual shows that are pure code, often squeezed into tiny file sizes. This is my small, ultra-old-school attempt at that idea.

## The reality: it's all just numbers

There is no picture of a storm anywhere in this project. It's all math:

- **Clouds** are noise functions: random numbers on a grid, smoothed and stacked at different sizes until they look like billowing cloud.
- **The lightning bolt** is a random walk: thousands of tiny steps, each turned by a random angle, with branches that fork off and fade out.
- **The flash** is computed per pixel. For every pixel, the code asks how much light gets from the lightning to that spot through the cloud.
- **Rain** is a grid of thin streaks, scrolled down the screen over time.
- **The rain sound** is thousands of tiny bursts of random numbers (each one a raindrop "tick"), plus a soft noise "wash" and a couple of drips.
- **The thunder** is physics. The code builds a 3D lightning channel, and every few meters of it sends a pressure pulse. Each pulse arrives at your ears after `distance ÷ 343 m/s` (the speed of sound). That's why you hear a crack first and then a long roll, and why far thunder is late and deep.

Your screen just shows the numbers. Your speakers just play the numbers.

## Controls

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| Click | Lightning where you click |
| **Enable mic** | Clap, knock or shout to trigger lightning |
| **H** | Settings: mic sensitivity, rain, clouds, render scale |
| **M** | Mute |
| **F** or **F11** | Fullscreen |

Tip: count the seconds between the flash and the thunder. Every 3 seconds is about 1 km.

## Run it locally

The microphone only works over `https://` or `localhost`, not when you double-click the file. In a terminal, from this folder:

```
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open http://localhost:8000 in Chrome or Edge.

## How it's built

- **WebGL2 fragment shader** (GLSL): sky, clouds, lighting, rain, tone mapping. Runs on the GPU for every pixel, every frame.
- **Canvas 2D**: draws the bolt once per strike, at three blur levels, as textures for the shader.
- **Web Audio API**: rain, thunder, echo, and the microphone.
- **Web Worker**: builds the thunder and rain sounds in the background, so the animation never stutters.

## Inspiration

My favourite demoscene production: [**Monolith** by Andromeda Software Development (ASD)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VD8c8XagJms&t=1s).

What blew my mind about demos like this: what you're watching isn't a video file. It's a program drawing every frame in real time, with the visuals generated and hand-tuned in code. In the tiniest demoscene categories (4 KB and 64 KB "intros"), even the music is a synthesizer written in code. Nothing is recorded; everything is computed.
